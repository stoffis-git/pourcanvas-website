## 1. Slip resistance (wet)

| Finding | Key |
|---|---|
| CCAA research cited by UNSW: wood-floated, broom and exposed-aggregate concrete "would usually achieve P5 or R13". The older classification is **V** (wet pendulum BPN > 54, COF > 0.59), described as very low slip risk. Applies to **new** surfaces only. | UNSW16 F |
| AS/NZS 4586 wet-pendulum classes: V > 54 BPN; W 45–54; X 35–44; Y 25–34; Z < 25. Class W was "consistently specified" for external pavements. | CCAA06 F |
| Stamped and broom finishes give similar slip resistance (micro-roughness governs). Broom is grippier **across** the grain, so broom perpendicular to walking direction. Abrasive-blasted finishes give variable results. Color pigment does not affect slip. | CCAA06 F |
| Sealers: penetrating sealers beat film-forming ones. One polished-concrete case measured BPN 35 with a coating sealer and 68 with a penetrating sealer. Steel-troweled concrete with a coating measured 21–36 ("unsatisfactory"). | CCAA06 F |
| Qualitative ranking (Concrete Decor): broom is the most slip resistant of the "smooth" finishes. Sandblasted is better than troweled. Wood float is better than steel trowel. Hard-troweled can be "fairly slippery". Stamped is better than smooth, depending on channel density. Film sealers reduce inherent slip resistance. | CD05 F |
| A slick stamped pool deck is usually caused by the **sealer**, not the stamp. Avoid flat patterns. Grit additives "improve the surface, but by no means" make it nonslip. Floated (sandy) stamped is less slick than troweled-smooth stamped. | CD13p F |
| Pool code: decks must be slip resistant. Where tested: wet pendulum ≥ P4 (AS 4586) or wet DCOF ≥ 0.42 (ANSI A326). | ISPSC24 S |
| ANSI A326.3 (2021/22) minimums: Interior Wet 0.42; Interior Wet Plus (incl. barefoot) 0.50; **Exterior Wet 0.55**. The standard is written for hard-surface flooring/tile. No published concrete-finish DCOF tables were found. | A326 F |
| Grit additives: aluminum oxide or trap rock broadcast into fresh concrete; polymer grit in sealer; polystyrene beads preferred on pool decks (gentler on feet). | CD05 F |

**Gap:** no US peer-reviewed DCOF/BPN table by finish (broom light/med/heavy, salt, sandblast levels) was found. Vendor COF numbers in the snippets (e.g. "light broom 0.5–0.6") were unsourced and are excluded.

## 2. Freeze-thaw and deicers

| Finding | Key |
|---|---|
| For slabs that are moist, freezing and exposed to deicers: 4000 psi (per ACI 332), air-entrained at **6%** total air for 3/4–1 in aggregate. "Avoid using steel trowels when finishing". "For most exterior surfaces a broom finish is adequate". Overworking the surface reduces entrained air. | NRMCA2 F |
| Deicers: none in the first winter (use sand). NaCl and CaCl₂ are acceptable in moderation. **Never** ammonium sulfate, ammonium nitrate or magnesium-based salts. Hose off salt tracked onto driveways. Seal with a breathable silane/siloxane (late summer). | NRMCA2 F |
| ACI exposure class F3 = freeze-thaw plus deicers (sidewalks and pavements). Target air 6–7.5% depending on aggregate size; w/cm ≤ 0.40–0.45 and f'c ≥ 4500–5000 psi depending on sub-class. | NRMCA/ACI guide, https://www.nrmca.org/wp-content/uploads/ACI_CI_NRMCA_Guide-to-Selecting_Exposure_Classes.pdf S |
| **Salt finish:** "In areas subject to freezing weather, water tends to collect in the indentations and freeze, potentially causing spalling", so it is more common in the warm West and South. The same page says good-quality concrete with a waterproofing sealer "should be durable enough to endure any climate". | CNsalt F |
| Salt finish is "not usually used for driveways or heavy traffic"; plows and freeze damage wear the texture. | BRK S |
| **Stamped:** freeze-thaw can damage it without proper mix and deicer precautions. Avoid deicers in the first winter. Ammonium products are especially harmful. Driveways in freeze-thaw areas need extra attention. | CNst F |
| Film sealers: "Too much surface water will break down a film-type sealer, turning colored concrete white". Keep 1/4 in/ft slope on imprinted surfaces to prevent puddling. The author prefers penetrating sealers in freeze-thaw. | CD13f F |
| **Exposed aggregate:** penetrating sealers last about 7–10 years but don't lock loose stones. Topical acrylic helps hold pebbles and reduces pitting but needs resealing every 12–36 months. | CD19 F |
| Snowblower or plow catching on exposed aggregate: only forum or contractor evidence exists. | weak |

## 3. Surface heat and barefoot comfort

| Finding | Key |
|---|---|
| New cement concrete SR 0.30–0.50; aged 0.20–0.35. New asphalt about 0.05. A light pavement segment measured about 17 °C (30 °F) cooler than an adjacent dark one in a thermal image. | LBNLcp F |
| LEED defaults: new gray concrete SR 0.35; new white 0.70. | PCA2982 S |
| Lab study: smooth-concrete albedo 0.41–0.77. Cement albedo has a "disproportionately strong influence". White-cement concrete scored 0.18–0.39 higher than gray. Wetting drops albedo by 0.23. Soiling and weathering cost 0.05–0.06. **After abrasion, albedo tracks the rock (aggregate) albedo**, so exposed-aggregate heat depends on stone color. | LBNL01 F |
| Integral color SR (medium gray cement): white pigment 0.52–0.59 (SRI 61–71); buff/tan/sand 0.37–0.42; brick red 0.36; dark tones (Redwood 0.22, Slate 0.22, Bark 0.18, Orchid 0.15, SRI 13–22). **Dark integral color roughly halves SR versus light tones.** | SOL F |
| Each +0.1 albedo lowers **peak surface** temperature by more than 3 °C. | REV24 S |
| Kool Deck "significantly lowers the surface temperature of concrete" (no number). Even darker Designer Colors are said to be "more comfortable than plain concrete". This is a **manufacturer claim**. | MTX F |
| Light spray-texture cool deck "will help quite a bit" (Las Vegas Q&A). Dark brick and tile get "extremely hot". No numbers. | CNcool F |
| Barefoot texture: broom "can be harsh on bare feet". Exposed aggregate is "too rough" for some people; mitigate with light exposure or 1/8 in polished stones. | CNbr F, CNea F |
| Color fade: integral color goes through the slab and won't wear away; acid and water-based stains are rated "excellent" for UV; **dyes are "not recommended for exterior use"** (some fade in sun). | CNcol F |

**Gap:** no measured surface-temperature comparison by finish texture. Texture effects on heat are unquantified; color, cement and aggregate dominate.

## 4. Maintenance

| Finish | Sealing / upkeep | Key |
|---|---|---|
| Broom | Penetrating sealer optional; seal in freeze-thaw. Traps dirt; can show color variation. | NRMCA2 F, CNbr F |
| Exposed aggregate | Penetrating sealer lasts about 7–10 years; acrylic every 12–36 months. Hides stains well. | CD19 F |
| Stamped | Clean and reseal every 2–3 years. Film sealer needs grit near pools; regrit at reseal. | CNst F, CD13p F |
| Salt | Seal after the pits dry fully; waterproofing sealer needed in cold areas. | CNsalt F |
| Sealers generally | Rougher textures are harder to clean. Grit additives wear out and need a new grit coat. | CD05 F, CCAA06 F |
| Sandblasted | Hard to maintain internally; external use is fine. No interval found. | CCAA06 F (weak) |

## 6. Typical best uses

| Finish | Driveway | Patio | Walkway | Pool deck | Key |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Broom | Default | Yes | Default | Yes (light broom; harsh barefoot) | NRMCA2 F, CNbr F, CNpool F |
| Exposed aggregate | Yes (slopes, stain hiding) | Yes | Yes | Yes, "highly slip resistant"; use small or polished stone | CNpool F, CNea F |
| Salt | Not usual | Yes (warm climates) | Yes | Yes, pool contractors favor it for nonskid texture | CNsalt F, BRK S |
| Stamped | Possible (freeze-thaw care) | Yes | Yes | Only with an aggressive texture, low-solids sealer and grit | CNst F, CD13p F |
| Smooth trowel | No (exterior) | Covered only | No | No | NRMCA2 F, CD05 F |
| Sandblast light/med/heavy | Retrofit texture | Yes | Yes | Light only (heavy is rough) | APA F, CCAA06 F |

Sandblast definitions (APA, F): **Light** removes surface skin just enough to show the coarse aggregate. **Medium** leaves roughly equal areas of aggregate and matrix. **Deep/heavy** leaves coarse aggregate dominant. Color shifts toward the aggregate as depth increases.

### Hard "avoid" rules

1. **Salt finish with freeze-thaw** (deicers or not): avoid for driveways and walks. A warning is acceptable only for a sealed patio. [CNsalt F, BRK S]
2. **Smooth steel trowel on any exterior surface** (driveway, walkway, patio, pool deck): avoid. [NRMCA2 F, CD05 F, CCAA06 F]
3. **Stamped smooth/seamless with film sealer on a pool deck:** avoid unless a grit additive is used and the pattern is aggressive. [CD13p F]
4. **Dye as exterior color:** avoid. [CNcol F]
5. **Dark integral color on a pool deck in hot sun:** warn, scoring −2 on heat. [SOL F, LBNLcp F]
6. **Any finish with ammonium or magnesium deicers, or with deicers in the first winter:** always warn. [NRMCA2 F, CNst F]
7. **Heavy broom or heavy sandblast on a pool deck:** warn about barefoot roughness. [CNbr F, CCAA06 F] ⚠
8. **Exposed aggregate on a plowed driveway in a snow climate:** warn about snowblower or plow edges. ⚠ forum evidence only
